## 2024-05-18 - Expandable Accordion Accessibility
**Learning:** Using an `onClick` on a non-interactive `div` for expandable sections (accordions) creates keyboard accessibility issues, as users cannot tab to the header and press Enter/Space to expand it. In `Experience.tsx`, a `div` wrapped the header content with a nested `button` for the icon, which was semantically incorrect and un-navigable.
**Action:** When implementing expandable UI patterns like accordions, use a full-width `<button>` for the entire clickable header area. Ensure you remove any nested interactive elements (like a secondary button inside it) and correctly apply `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls` to the main button to provide clear screen reader support and natural keyboard navigation.
## 2023-10-27 - Skip-Link Focus Management & Expandable ARIA Controls
**Learning:** For a skip-to-content link to function correctly for keyboard and screen reader users, the target container must be programmatically focusable (`tabIndex={-1}`). Without this, some browsers will visually scroll to the anchor but won't move the actual keyboard focus, causing the next "Tab" press to reset back to the top of the page. Additionally, manually-implemented expandable sections (like the custom accordions and "view all" toggles in this codebase) frequently miss the structural `aria-controls` attribute, which is crucial for screen readers to understand the relationship between the toggle button and the content it reveals.
**Action:** Always ensure that the target of a skip-link (typically the `<main>` element) has `tabIndex={-1}` and `className="focus:outline-none"` (to avoid an ugly focus ring when clicked). For any custom expand/collapse components, consistently implement the `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls` pair on the trigger button, mapping to a unique `id` on the content container.
